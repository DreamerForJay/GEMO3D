Abstract

Monocular 3D vehicle detection requires estimating object depth from a single image. The height of a detected 2D bounding box differs from the projected height of the vehicle’s 3D centerline, introducing a depth bias that varies with viewing angle and vehicle shape. GEMO3D combines learned vehicle detection, classification and orientation estimation with camera geometry to compensate for this discrepancy.

A vehicle-specific rank-1 model estimates the projection-height ratio from the viewing angle and initial pinhole depth. The corrected depth is used to reconstruct the 3D box, followed by ground-plane constraints and reprojection refinement. On 165 vehicle instances matched by all four evaluated methods in a CARLA sequence, GEMO3D achieves a mean 3D center error of 0.25 m and a mean BEV IoU of 0.82.

Method

YOLOv11 detects the vehicle and a classifier selects its dimension prior. Ego-Net estimates the viewing angle. Camera calibration and the detected box height provide an initial depth estimate; the height-ratio model then corrects this estimate before 3D reconstruction.

Projection-Height Ratio Compensation

Let H be the vehicle height, fy the vertical focal length, and h2D the detected box height. Initial and corrected depth are given by

Zraw = fy H / h2D
Zcorr = Zraw · r̂c(θ, Zraw)

The class-specific function r̂c approximates h2D / h3D,proj. It is fitted over viewing angle and depth using rank-1 SVD. Ground-truth depth is not required at inference.
